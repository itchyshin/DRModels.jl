- **A bare formula and R's `x1:x2` now get named refusals.** `drm(@formula(y ~ x), Gaussian(); data)` (no `bf()`) used to fail with `MethodError: no method matching drm(::FormulaTerm, ::Gaussian; ...)`. It now throws an `ArgumentError` that says the formula must be created with `bf()` (as drmTMB does) and shows the call to write, for every family and for the bivariate and mixed-family routes. A formula with `:` at the top of a right-hand side, such as `y ~ x1 + x2 + x1:x2`, used to fail with `Cannot convert an object of type StepRangeLen ... to Float64`, because Julia reads `:` as a range. It now throws an `ArgumentError` that shows how Julia read the term and names the spellings that work: `a & b` (interaction only) or `a * b` (main effects and interaction). Not covered: `poly()`, `I()` and `splines::ns()` fail before `drm` is called, and a `:` inside a function call (for example `log(x1:x2)`) is not detected.
