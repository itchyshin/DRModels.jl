- **Docs: `predict` sets random effects to zero, and the default marginal approximation is named (#1092, #1070).** The `predict` and `predict_parameters` docstrings said random effects were "integrated out", and the `fitted` docstring called its value the "population/marginal mean". On a random-effect fit all three return the population-level value with every random effect at zero (`g⁻¹(Xβ̂)` for most families), the prediction for an average group. The docstrings now say so, give the log-link marginal mean (`predict(fit, newdata) .* exp(re_sd(fit)[:g]^2 / 2)`), and note that drmTMB's `predict(newdata)` returns the same value while its `fitted()` adds the random-effect modes. `test/test_predict_random_effects.jl` pins the behaviour on Poisson and Bernoulli `(1 | g)` fits. The drmTMB-parity and Getting-started pages now say that the default `marginal = :LA` uses each route's own integrator: per-group adaptive Gauss–Hermite quadrature with 5 nodes for an ordinary `(1 | g)` on most non-Gaussian means (21 for Tweedie, 41 for CumulativeLogit), a fixed 32-node grid for Gaussian `sigma ~ (1 | g)` and ZeroOneBeta, and the Laplace approximation on crossed and structured routes. They also say that `marginal = :Laplace` reproduces drmTMB (on a Poisson `(1 | g)` comparison, a 0.0014 log-likelihood gap fell to 5.6e-9), that it is for parity rather than a better default, and that log-likelihoods, likelihood-ratio tests and AIC/BIC compare only within one approximation. Descriptions of the ordinary `(1 | g)` default as a fixed "32-node" grid, superseded by adaptive quadrature in v0.7.2, were corrected in `families.md`, `capabilities.md`, `rosetta.md`, the Laplace-vs-variational guide and the Poisson/Beta/Gamma/NegBinomial2 docstrings.
