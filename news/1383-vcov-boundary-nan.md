- **`vcov` and `stderror` give `NaN`, not a made-up number, at a coordinate the fit flags as a boundary (#1383).** When the Hessian guard flags a coordinate as numerically flat (for example a random-effect SD at zero, or the size of a negative binomial fitted to Poisson data), that coordinate is dropped before the rest of the Hessian is inverted, and its row and column of `vcov(fit)` are `NaN`. Before, the pseudo-inverse inverted finite-difference noise there and could return a variance of -1e8 (a Binomial `(1 | g)` fit with `marginal = :Laplace` on 20 rows), so `sqrt.(diag(vcov(fit)))` threw a `DomainError`. The other coordinates keep their covariances, and `bridge_diagnostics(fit).boundary` lists the flagged ones. `stderror` is `NaN` at a flagged coordinate (it was `Inf`, or a finite number such as 3153 on the bivariate structured fit with a rank-one among-group covariance), and so is its Wald interval and its entry in `coeftable`. The two conventions are kept apart: `Inf` for complete separation (the estimate itself diverges), for the coefficient of an aliased design column, and for every coordinate when the covariance was skipped (`se = false`) or failed; `NaN` for a coordinate flagged at a boundary. When every coordinate is flagged, every standard error is `NaN`; the fit stores that flag (`uncertainty` is `:boundary_all`), so it stays different from `se = false` and from a failed covariance. `bias_correct` of a quantity that uses a flagged coordinate gives `NaN` (not `Inf`). `predict(...; se = true)`, `predict_parameters`, `bias_correct`, the cross-family correlation interval, and `repeatability`, `icc` and `heritability` with `newdata` keep their standard errors for a row or quantity that does not use the flagged coordinate; one that does gets `NaN`. Fits with no flagged coordinate are unchanged.

  **The flag does not depend on units.** A coefficient on a covariate, a location coefficient of a Gaussian, Student or skew-normal fit (units of the response), and an off-diagonal covariance entry carry units, so their curvature changes with the units of the data, and a covariate in units of 1e5 used to make every other coordinate look flat. A coefficient on a covariate in a log or logit block (for example the slope of `sigma`) is judged per unit of its own design column: its curvature divided by the squared norm of the column is flagged when it is at most 3e-8. Any other coordinate that carries units is flagged only when its curvature is exactly zero. Every other coordinate (log SDs, log sizes, log scales, correlations, intercepts of log and logit blocks) is flagged when its curvature is at most 3e-8 times the number of observations; it used to be 3e-8 times the largest curvature among the others, which made a well identified log scale look flat when the spread of the data was 1e-4 or less (a LogNormal or Gamma with a CV of 1e-5). The `drm` entry applies this once the fit is built, and it can only remove a flag that the matrix alone would have set. A well identified Gaussian fit with a covariate multiplied by 1e5 used to get `NaN` standard errors and status `:degenerate` (and a warning about a variance boundary); it now gets finite standard errors that scale exactly with the covariate, no warning, and `:converged`. An aliased design column plus a covariate in large units no longer gives `NaN` standard errors to the identified intercepts: only the aliased coefficient is flagged. Called on a bare matrix, `DRModels._vcov_from_hessian` still compares each curvature with the largest one. Limits: very small weights or frequencies make every curvature small and are read as flatness, and a flat slope whose design column was not recorded (a joint missing-data fit) is flagged only at exactly zero curvature.

  Four side effects to know about. (1) `convergence_status` changes from `:degenerate` to `:boundary` for a fit whose only flagged coordinates are random-effect log SDs at or below the sparse-Laplace floor of -8 (a `:resd` block), the same case the floor rule of #1190 already handles. Before, the covariance of such a fit kept a variance of the order of 1e-20 to 1e-60 for the collapsed SD, and whether the status rules on a finite, positive definite covariance passed depended on the sign of a smallest eigenvalue of that size. So the class is every fit with a random-effect SD estimated at zero and nothing else flagged: a Gaussian `y ~ x + (1 | g)` fit with group means of the residual at exactly zero, a Gamma `(1 | g)` fit (five of eight simulated sets), a Poisson `(1 | g)` fit with a mean of exp(8), and the 20-row Binomial and Poisson fits with `marginal = :Laplace`. Their status is now `:boundary` whenever the covariance of the other coordinates is finite, positive definite and not inflated. Any other flagged coordinate leaves the status at `:degenerate`, because the covariance is then not finite. (2) `issymmetric(vcov(fit))` is `false` for a fit with a flagged coordinate (`NaN != NaN`), and `eigvals(Symmetric(vcov(fit)))` throws `ArgumentError: matrix contains Infs or NaNs`; call them on the unflagged block, `V[keep, keep]` with `keep = setdiff(axes(V, 1), bridge_diagnostics(fit).boundary)`. When every coordinate is flagged, `vcov` is now all `NaN` (it was all zeros). (3) `repeatability`, `icc` and `heritability` with `newdata` (`src/heritability.jl`) lost the standard error of every row when one coordinate was `NaN`, because `0 * NaN` is `NaN`; a row that does not use the flagged coordinate now keeps its standard error. (4) The status still applies the drmTMB rule on inflated standard errors, so a covariate in units of 1e-5 (a slope standard error in the thousands) gives `:degenerate` without any flagged coordinate, as in drmTMB.
