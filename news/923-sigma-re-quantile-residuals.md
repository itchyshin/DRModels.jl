- **Fix: quantile residuals of a Gaussian `sigma ~ 1 + (1 | g)` fit no longer treat the scale random intercept as a mean intercept (#923).** `residuals(fit; type = :quantile)` sent every fit with exactly one random-intercept SD to the σ_b-marginal route (#760), whatever axis the intercept was on. A scale-only fit therefore integrated the MEAN over N(0, τ̂²), with τ̂ the log-σ random-effect SD, which is a different model: on the new test's simulated fit (τ = 0.6, 30 groups of 20) the residual SD was 0.85 instead of about 1, and residuals sat up to 1.22 from the correct reference.
  - A lone log-σ intercept is now integrated out of the scale, σ_i e^b with b ~ N(0, τ̂²), on the same 32 Gauss–Hermite nodes. On that fit the residuals match an adaptive-quadrature reference to 1.4e-6 and their SD is 1.01.
  - The routing keys on the `<g>_logsigma` name of the random-effect SD. A lone mean intercept (`(1 | g)`, `phylo(1 | g)`, `relmat(1 | g)`) keeps the σ_b-marginal route, and a mean plus sigma `(1 | g)` pair keeps both random effects at 0. A non-Gaussian σ-axis intercept also keeps b = 0, because no σ-marginal mapping is verified for those families.
  - drmTMB's quantile residuals for these fits condition on the fitted modes instead: its `predict()` adds them, and its residuals equal `(y − μ̂)/σ̂` with the log-σ mode included to 4e-15. DRModels.jl keeps the population-level reference it already uses for the mean intercept, so per-row values differ from drmTMB's. Of the two population-level choices, the σ-marginal is the closer to drmTMB: on one simulated fit its correlation with drmTMB's residuals is 0.94 (largest difference 1.61), against 0.91 (2.32) with the random effect set to 0.
