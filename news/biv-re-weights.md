- **Prior weights on the bivariate Gaussian with `(1 | p | g)`.** `drm(bf(mu1 = y1 ~ x1 + (1 | p | g), mu2 = y2 ~ x2 + (1 | p | g), ...), Gaussian(); data, weights = w)` now fits, ML and REML, as drmTMB does. A weight multiplies the whole row (the joint density of both responses): an integer weight equals repeating the row, a zero weight equals dropping it, and `nobs` still counts rows. Rows with a missing predictor or response drop together with their weights. Measured against drmTMB 0.8.1 on 9 cells (log-likelihood within 1e-4, fixed effects within 1e-3 of the R SE, log SDs and correlation within 5e-3, standard errors within 1e-2). Negative, non-finite, missing, wrong-length or all-zero weights are refused. With missing predictor or response values, give one weight per row of `data`: a vector already cut to the kept rows is refused with a clear error that states the row count of `data` (it used to be accepted and gave a wrong log-likelihood). On a REML fit, `confint(method = :profile)` is refused because the stored objective is the ML likelihood (use Wald intervals, the bootstrap, or an ML fit). `update()` on a weighted fit of this kind now needs `weights =` stated (`weights = weights(fit)` to keep them), and the parametric bootstrap refits keep the weights.  After the numerical fix in the same series (no `DomainError` on ordinary data), the weighted fit no longer aborts on about half of ordinary simulated data sets: 0 failures in 60 for each of four weight patterns and for REML (it was 22 to 31 in 60), and a weighted parametric bootstrap runs through.
